About Origin

In Dan Brown’s novel Origin, Edmond Kirsch answers “where do we come from?” with a simulation that fast-forwards nature. This is an honest version: one slider from the Big Bang to today, following matter as it organises itself at ever smaller scales. The idea connecting every chapter: energy flows, and matter organises into structure over time.

No computer can simulate particles evolving into people, and we don’t pretend to. Every chapter carries a badge: SIMULATED real physics or a real model runs live; MODELLED a simplified model tuned to look right; RECONSTRUCTED a visual driven by scientific dates, not by simulation. Where we can simulate, we simulate. Where nobody can, we show the science honestly.

CONTROLLED / RELEASED. CONTROLLED mode removes randomness and starts from a neat, designed layout. It shows that in our models, structure needs both randomness and a flow of energy. It is a thought experiment, not a claim about what a real universe could be. Details: chapter 2 starts from a perfect grid with no ripples (no stars form); chapter 6 starts from a neat lattice already settled into its rest state, with no noise and the energy on (it only vibrates in place); chapter 7's creatures are identical and never mutate (no new species). Chapters 3, 4, 5 and 8 are cards, because nothing formed for them to build on. Tested honestly: a neat solar-system disk run on its own still collides into planets (its orbits are packed tightly and the bodies pull on each other), which is why chapter 4 follows the story's logic (no stars, no Sun) instead.

  1. The Hot Beginning (MODELLED): an opaque glowing fog whose colour follows the textbook temperature (T ∝ t−1/2), clearing at recombination to reveal the first light. The clearing is drawn starting earlier than it really did (from about 57,000 years, versus about 250,000 in reality) so it is visible on the log clock. The CMB map is a random field coloured by seed density (denser = slightly colder), not the real Planck map. The particle soup arrives in phase 6.
  2. The Cosmic Web and First Stars (SIMULATED): real gravity (2D particle-mesh, 16,384 particles) in an expanding universe, started from the very ripples shown as the CMB. 2D, so qualitative. The ripple amplitude was tuned so the first star ignites within the 100–200 million year estimate. With no ripples, nothing forms.
  3. A Galaxy Takes Shape (MODELLED): a picture built from equations, not a gravity simulation. A puffy cloud settles into a thin disk; a flat rotation curve (230 km/s) makes inner stars lap outer ones; the spiral arms are a density wave (two arms, 12° pitch) that stars drive through like a traffic jam, lighting up gas as young blue stars; a smaller galaxy (Gaia-Enceladus) falls in. Rotation is drawn about 13× slower than real; dates of the merger and the thin disk (debated) come from the facts table. The real Milky Way also has a bar and minor arms.
  4. Birth of the Solar System (SIMULATED): real 2D gravity and collisions between 800 planetesimals that merge into a few planets, one of which becomes Earth. Time is compressed (about 600 orbits for 10–100 million years), collision radii are 200× real and the bodies pull on each other 200× harder than real. The gas disk's glow is scripted, and the Moon-forming impact is an illustration (RECONSTRUCTED).
  5. Young Earth (RECONSTRUCTED): a 3D globe whose surface (lava, oceans, haze, impacts) follows the dated events, not a climate or geology model. Continents are procedural, and their slow re-shaping is a visual stand-in for drift, not plate tectonics.
  6. The Origin of Life (SIMULATED): the live driven-particle simulation. Driven matter organises faster and further than the undriven control (order: driven wins in 93% of 1,200 sweep runs). This illustrates Jeremy England’s debated hypothesis; it does not create life. The particles, bonds and cluster outlines are drawn from the simulation (same colour = same composition and shape; the control actually has more look-alikes, which is why there is no "repeated structures" chart). Decoration only, not simulated: the glow and trails, the travelling light bands (a picture of the field's strength and direction), the warm vent glow, bubbles, dust, the microscope zooms and the optional hum.
  7. Evolution: from cells to humans (RECONSTRUCTED, with a SIMULATED toy model). First a toy model of natural selection (genes, mutation, competition; real dates of oxygen rises, Snowball Earth, the Cambrian and two mass extinctions drive its environment; a no-oxygen control stays single-celled). Then our own lineage morphs through 24 key stages, from first cells to Homo sapiens. The key stages come from the fossil record and real groups (dates and sources in src/data/sources.md); the in-between frames are illustrations, and the drawings are our own simple schematics, all shown at the same size with a real-size scale bar. Living relatives branch off the family tree as cousins: monkeys and apes are not our ancestors, we share ancestors with them. Brain sizes are fossil measurements. The toy model's numbers were tuned so its mechanism shows clearly.
  8. Humans (RECONSTRUCTED): dated milestones, published world-population estimates (Our World in Data, UN) and city lights on the globe.
