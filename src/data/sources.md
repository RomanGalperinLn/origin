# Sources for lineage.json and brainSize.json

All values were checked against the sources below in October 2026. Dates are years ago (Ga = billion, Ma = million, ka = thousand). "TimeTree" values are the pairwise median divergence times (and confidence intervals) that the TimeTree 5 database returned for *Homo sapiens* against a representative species. Where TimeTree's "adjusted" and "precomputed" medians differed, the adjusted value was used, because it is kept consistent across the whole tree.

## Source list

- **S1** Smithsonian NMNH Human Origins Program, "Sahelanthropus tchadensis". https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/sahelanthropus-tchadensis. Supports: lived 7-6 Ma; brain slightly smaller than a chimpanzee's; hominins split from apes 7-6 Ma.
- **S2** Smithsonian Human Origins, "Ardipithecus ramidus". https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/ardipithecus-ramidus. Supports: about 4.4 Ma; "Ardi" about 120 cm and 50 kg; grasping (divergent) big toe.
- **S3** Smithsonian Human Origins, "Australopithecus afarensis". https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/australopithecus-afarensis. Supports: 3.85-2.95 Ma; Lucy 3.2 Ma; males 151 cm, females 105 cm; brain usually under 500 cc.
- **S4** Smithsonian Human Origins, "Homo habilis". https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-habilis. Supports: 2.4-1.4 Ma (youngest fossil 1.44 Ma); height 100-135 cm; braincase slightly larger than Australopithecus.
- **S5** Smithsonian Human Origins, "Homo erectus". https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-erectus. Supports: about 1.89 Ma to 110 ka; height 145-185 cm.
- **S6** Smithsonian Human Origins, "Homo heidelbergensis". https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-heidelbergensis. Supports: 700-200 ka; average height 175 cm (males) and 157 cm (females); wooden spears; Neanderthal and modern-human lines split 400-350 ka (this page's figure).
- **S7** Smithsonian Human Origins, "Homo neanderthalensis". https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-neanderthalensis. Supports: 400-40 ka; brains as large as ours or larger; shared ancestor with us 700-300 ka.
- **S8** Smithsonian Human Origins, "Homo sapiens". https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-sapiens. Supports: about 300 ka to present; average brain about 1,300 cc.
- **S9** Smithsonian Human Origins, "Brains" (Human Characteristics). https://humanorigins.si.edu/human-characteristics/brains. Supports: average adult brain mass 384 g (chimpanzee) and 1,352 g (human); brain size roughly tripled over human evolution.
- **S10** Australian Museum, "Sahelanthropus tchadensis" (2024 update). https://australian.museum/learn/science/human-evolution/sahelanthropus-tchadensis/. Supports: 6-7 Ma; brain 320-380 cc; probably chimpanzee-sized.
- **S11** Australian Museum, "Ardipithecus ramidus". https://australian.museum/learn/science/human-evolution/ardipithecus-ramidus/. Supports: 4.4-4.2 Ma; brain about 300-350 cc; Ardi about 120 cm, 50 kg.
- **S12** Australian Museum, "Australopithecus afarensis". https://australian.museum/learn/science/human-evolution/australopithecus-afarensis/. Supports: 3.9-2.8 Ma; brain averaging about 430 cc; females 105-110 cm, males about 150 cm.
- **S13** Australian Museum, "Homo habilis". https://australian.museum/learn/science/human-evolution/homo-habilis/. Supports: 2.3-1.5 Ma; brain averaging 610 cc, individual skulls about 500 cc (KNM-ER 1813) to 750-800 cc (KNM-ER 1470, which many researchers assign to *H. rudolfensis*).
- **S14** Australian Museum, "Homo ergaster" (the African early form of *H. erectus* in the broad sense). https://australian.museum/learn/science/human-evolution/homo-ergaster/. Supports: 1.9-1.5 Ma core range; brain averaging about 860 cc; Dmanisi skulls 600-780 cc.
- **S15** Australian Museum, "Homo erectus" (Asian *H. erectus* in the narrow sense). https://australian.museum/learn/science/human-evolution/homo-erectus/. Supports: brain averaging about 1,050 cc; species lasted to about 100 ka.
- **S16** Australian Museum, "Homo heidelbergensis". https://australian.museum/learn/science/human-evolution/homo-heidelbergensis/. Supports: 600-300 ka (some fossils to 800 ka); brain averaging about 1,250 cc; Kabwe 1 is 1,280 cc.
- **S17** Australian Museum, "Homo neanderthalensis". https://australian.museum/learn/science/human-evolution/homo-neanderthalensis/. Supports: brain averaging 1,500 cc; Amud 1 is 1,740 cc, the largest fossil human brain known.
- **S18** Kumar S. et al. (2022) "TimeTree 5: an expanded resource for species divergence times", *Molecular Biology and Evolution* 39: msac174. Values queried from http://timetree.org/api/pairwise/9606/{taxon} (e.g. https://timetree.org/api/pairwise/9606/9598). Supports every molecular divergence time for cousins (see table).
- **S19** Moody E.R.R. et al. (2024) "The nature of the last universal common ancestor and its impact on the early Earth system", *Nature Ecology & Evolution* (doi:10.1038/s41559-024-02461-1). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11383801/. Supports: LUCA (bacteria/archaea root) about 4.2 Ga (4.09-4.33 Ga). Earlier studies gave about 3.5-3.8 Ga; the age is still debated.
- **S20** Natural History Museum, London (2022) press release "Earth's oldest stromatolites help in the search for ancient life", on a 2022 study in *Geology*. https://www.nhm.ac.uk/press-office/press-releases/earth-s-oldest-stromatolites-help-in-the-search-for-ancient-life.html. Supports: stromatolites in the 3.48 Ga Dresser Formation, Pilbara, Western Australia. (Claims of about 3.7 Ga from Greenland are debated, as events.json already notes.)
- **S21** Miao L., Yin Z., Knoll A.H., Qu Y., Zhu M. (2024) "1.63-billion-year-old multicellular eukaryotes from the Chuanlinggou Formation in North China", *Science Advances* (2024). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10807817/ and NIGPAS press release https://english.nigpas.cas.cn/new/hs/rp/202401/t20240124_656672.html. Supports: oldest unambiguous eukaryote fossils about 1.65 Ga; oldest multicellular eukaryotes 1.63 Ga.
- **S22** Gibson T.M. et al. (2018) "Precise age of *Bangiomorpha pubescens* dates the origin of eukaryotic photosynthesis", *Geology* 46: 135-138. https://authors.library.caltech.edu/83811. Supports: oldest multicellular fossil of a living group (red alga) 1.047 Ga (1.06-1.03 Ga).
- **S23** Yin Z. et al. (2015) "Sponge grade body fossil with cellular resolution dating 60 Myr before the Cambrian", *PNAS* 112: E1453-E1460. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4378401. Supports: *Eocyathispongia qiania*, about 600 Ma, about 1.2 mm wide (single specimen; its sponge identity is not universally accepted).
- **S24** Evans S.D. et al. (2020) "Discovery of the oldest bilaterian from the Ediacaran of South Australia", *PNAS* 117: 7845-7850. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7149385. Supports: *Ikaria wariootia*, older than 555 Ma, 2-7 mm long, with distinct front and back ends.
- **S25** Shu D.-G. et al. (1999) "Lower Cambrian vertebrates from south China", *Nature* 402: 42-46 (Chengjiang biota, about 518 Ma). Supports: *Haikouichthys* and *Myllokunmingia*, jawless, about 2.5-3 cm long. (Age as cited by secondary summaries; one older summary gives about 525-530 Ma.)
- **S26** Zhu M. et al. (2022) "The oldest complete jawed vertebrates from the early Silurian of China", *Nature* 609: 954-958 (one of four linked papers on the Chongqing and Guizhou sites). https://english.cas.cn/newsroom/research_news/life/202209/t20220928_320888.shtml. Supports: complete jawed fish about 436 Ma, sites 439-436 Ma; *Xiushanosteus* about 3 cm long.
- **S27** Daeschler E.B., Shubin N.H., Jenkins F.A. (2006) "A Devonian tetrapod-like fish and the evolution of the tetrapod body plan", *Nature* 440: 757-763. Size from CBC News coverage (2006): specimens 1-2.5 m. Supports: *Tiktaalik*, about 375 Ma, neck and wrist-like fin bones.
- **S28** Clack J.A., "Acanthostega", Tree of Life Web Project (http://tolweb.org/Acanthostega/15016), and Porro L.B., Rayfield E.J., Clack J.A. (2015) *PLOS ONE* (3D skull reconstruction), with press coverage giving adult length about 60 cm. Supports: Famennian of East Greenland (about 365-360 Ma), eight digits, largely aquatic.
- **S29** Niedzwiedzki G. et al. (2010) "Tetrapod trackways from the early Middle Devonian period of Poland", *Nature* 463: 43-48. Supports: trackways about 390 Ma (sources say 395-391 Ma), suggesting four-legged animals well before the oldest body fossils. Still debated.
- **S30** Mann A., Gee B.M., Pardo J.D. et al. (2020) on *Asaphestera*, *Papers in Palaeontology* (doi:10.1002/spp2.1316); Carleton University news summary https://carleton.ca/news/story/fossil-relative-modern-mammals/. Supports: oldest definitive synapsid, about 318-315 Ma at Joggins, Nova Scotia; *Archaeothyris* (about 306 Ma) is the oldest well-known synapsid.
- **S31** Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Thrinaxodon". https://www.britannica.com/animal/Thrinaxodon (page blocked our fetch; content taken from search excerpts). Supports: Early Triassic (about 250 Ma) cynodont, about 0.5 m long; burrow cast and semi-sprawling posture from other summaries.
- **S32** Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Morganucodon" (Britannica student edition: https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Morganucodon/576969). Supports: early mammal or close mammal relative from about 205 Ma; 27-89 g; probably nocturnal and insect-eating.
- **S33** Florida Museum of Natural History (2018) "Oldest primates in North America", on Morse P.E. et al. (2019) *Journal of Human Evolution*. https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/science/oldest-primates-north-america. Supports: mouse-sized *Teilhardina*, about 56 Ma, during the Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum.
- **S34** Gebo D.L. et al. (2000), *Nature* 404: 276 (Eosimias ankle bones, Carnegie Museum and IVPP Beijing); Smithsonian Magazine, "Out of Asia: how monkey and ape ancestors colonized Africa". Supports: *Eosimias* from China, about 45 Ma (an older report said about 40 Ma), one of the earliest anthropoids, about 100-150 g.
- **S35** Zalmout I.S. et al. (2010) "New Oligocene primate from Saudi Arabia and the divergence of apes and Old World monkeys", *Nature* 466: 360-364. Supports: *Saadanius*, 29-28 Ma, stem catarrhine of 15-20 kg; monkey-ape split between 29-28 and 24 Ma. (*Aegyptopithecus*, an earlier stem catarrhine, is about 33 Ma.)
- **S36** McNulty K.P. et al. (2015) "A systematic revision of *Proconsul* with the description of a new genus of early Miocene hominoid", *Journal of Human Evolution* (2015); Michel L.A. et al. (2014) *Nature Communications* 5: 3236 (closed-canopy forest at Rusinga). Supports: tailless early Miocene apes in Kenya, about 20-17 Ma, forest habitat.
- **S37** Lebatard A.-E. et al. (2008) "Cosmogenic nuclide dating of *Sahelanthropus tchadensis* and *Australopithecus bahrelghazali*", *PNAS* 105: 3226-3231. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2265126. Supports: Toumai skull 7.2-6.8 Ma.
- **S38** Langergraber K.E. et al. (2012) "Generation times in wild chimpanzees and gorillas suggest earlier divergence times in great ape and human evolution", *PNAS* 109: 15716-15721. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3465451. Supports: human-chimp split at least 7-8 Ma; Neanderthal-modern split 800-400 ka.
- **S39** Prufer K. et al. (2014) "The complete genome sequence of a Neanderthal from the Altai Mountains", *Nature* 505: 43-49. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4031459. Supports: modern-human vs Neanderthal/Denisovan population split 589-553 ka (PSMC) or 765-550 ka (second method); both depend on a debated mutation rate.
- **S40** Herries A.I.R. et al. (2020) "Contemporaneity of *Australopithecus*, *Paranthropus*, and early *Homo erectus* in South Africa", *Science* 368: eaaw7293. Supports: earliest *H. erectus*-like cranium DNH 134, 2.04-1.95 Ma.
- **S41** Richter D. et al. (2017) "The age of the hominin fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco, and the origins of the Middle Stone Age", *Nature* 546: 293-296. Supports: oldest *H. sapiens* fossils 315 +/- 34 ka.
- **S42** Gunz P. et al. (2020) "*Australopithecus afarensis* endocasts suggest ape-like brain organization and prolonged brain growth", *Science Advances* 6: eaaz4729. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7112758. Supports: *A. afarensis* adult average 445 cc (individual skulls 365-526 cc; Lucy about 388 cc); chimpanzee mean 369 cc (SD 37, N = 240).
- **S43** Rightmire G.P. (2004) "Brain size and encephalization in early to mid-Pleistocene *Homo*", *American Journal of Physical Anthropology* 124: 109-123. Supports: *H. erectus* mean 973 cc (30 skulls); Middle Pleistocene (*H. heidelbergensis*) mean 1,206 cc (10 skulls).
- **S44** Lordkipanidze D. et al. (2013) "A complete skull from Dmanisi, Georgia, and the evolutionary biology of early *Homo*", *Science* 342: 326-331. Supports: Dmanisi Skull 5, about 546 cc, about 1.8 Ma; the authors place Dmanisi in *H. erectus* (others disagree).
- **S45** Arsuaga J.L. et al. (1993) "Three new human skulls from the Sima de los Huesos Middle Pleistocene site in Sierra de Atapuerca, Spain", *Nature* 362: 534-537. Supports: adult endocranial volumes 1,125 and 1,390 cc.
- **S46** De Caro L. (2024) "Commentary: Human brains have shrunk: the questions are when and why", *Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution* 12: 1368347. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2024.1368347/full. Supports: living humans 1,349 +/- 78 cc (Beals et al. 1984, 5,288 skulls); individuals differ by more than 1,000 cc (Holloway et al. 2004); later Neanderthals 1,459 +/- 182 cc (DeSilva et al. 2023 data).
- **S47** Wikipedia, "List of Neanderthal fossils" (secondary source, used only for one specimen value). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Neanderthal_fossils. Supports: Gibraltar 1 (adult female) about 1,200 cc.

### scaleCm (on-screen sizes)

These come from the sources where available: Ikaria 2-7 mm (S24), Eocyathispongia 1.2 mm (S23), Haikouichthys 2.5 cm (S25), Xiushanosteus 3 cm (S26), Tiktaalik 1-2.5 m (S27), Acanthostega 60 cm (S28), Thrinaxodon 50 cm (S31), Morganucodon about 10 cm (S32), Ardi 120 cm (S2), afarensis 105-151 cm (S3), habilis 100-135 cm (S4), erectus 145-185 cm (S5), heidelbergensis 157-175 cm (S6). The rest are rough typical sizes for the group, not taken from a source: cells 2 um and 50 um, early many-celled filaments 1 mm, synapsid 50 cm, Teilhardina and Eosimias about 8-10 cm (mouse-sized, 30-150 g), Saadanius about 60 cm, Proconsul about 70 cm, chimp LCA and Sahelanthropus about 100 cm (chimp-sized), modern human 165 cm.
